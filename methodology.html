The pitch

This walks a homeowner from "what is this letter" to "here's your deadline, here's who to call, here's the paperwork" — free where it matters, with a paid layer that doesn't touch survival.
  • What's real: everything you can click. The deadline math runs live, the letters generate, the phone numbers are verified — and the AI extractor read 29 real scanned notices, with 27 exact sale-date matches and two results refused for human review.
  • Why it's built this way: code computes every date, attorneys own every legal sentence (the production contract — this demo's templates await that review), AI works only at the edges.
  • What the newsroom would measure: completed answers, source-document clicks, legal-aid referrals, saved-notice reminder opt-ins, returning users, subscriber conversions and a public corrections count — all real events this page already structures, none fabricated here.

Exhibit — plain-language translation, and the error human citation review caught

The same real notice (FRCL-2026-2290), translated under the no-citation-no-sentence rule: every plain sentence must carry its basis from the document or statute. Four sentences came back — three clean, and one where the AI's plain sentence contradicts its own citation. That row is the whole design argument.

Why the flagged row is the point: because every sentence must carry its basis, a reviewer comparing each sentence with its cited source catches the model saying 10:00 when the quote says 11:00 — and that row routes to a human instead of a reader. That is Meeting Monitor's discrepancy-checker pattern, demonstrated on a live error, on a real document, on the first try. A time-of-sale error in a foreclosure tool is exactly the class of mistake this architecture exists to stop.

I returned to full-time employment on August 3 and am able to resume regular monthly payments. However, I am unable to pay the entire missed amount in a lump sum at this time."

The model also returned a facts-used ledger (six claims, each traceable to the input), and an automated token check (dates, numbers) plus line-by-line review confirmed no date, number or circumstance appears in the output that wasn't in the reader's words. Same dignity, better sentences, nothing invented — the reader approves every line before it goes anywhere. The live demo's server gate checks the same token categories; the reader's approval covers the rest.

The document desk — the full product design

Six documents in the production design. Three of them (the brief, the call script and the hardship narrative) run live in the working demo on the tool page; the rest are designed, not built. Survival documents stay free — the paid layer is proof-of-submission, not paperwork.

Always free

Notice-to-Action Brief

One page: what your notice says, its printed dates and amounts, the questions to resolve and the documents to gather. Unknown fields stay marked UNKNOWN.

Always free

Servicer call script

A three-minute script of questions: is a sale scheduled, what does loss mitigation require, where does it go, how do I get written confirmation. Questions only — no promised outcomes.

Always free

Loss-mitigation transmittal packet

Cover letter, enclosure index and receipt log built from the servicer's own checklist. Says "I am submitting the following" — never "my application is complete."

Always free

Hardship narrative builder

A guided, plain-facts narrative: what happened, what changed, what you're asking for. No embellishment, no invented numbers; you approve every sentence.

Always free

Legal-aid intake brief

Two pages a lawyer can act on in minutes: chronology, stage, document index, urgent dates, your questions. The handoff, made effortless.

Paid layer

Private evidence binder

Indexed PDF binder, communication log, proof-of-submission vault with delivery tracking, and a redacted sharing copy. The "prove what I sent" problem, solved — this is the product's honest upsell.

The other clocks: property tax and HOA

The main page's calculator covers the mortgage / trustee-sale track. These two are completely different legal processes with their own timelines. General legal information, not legal advice, and not a substitute for a Texas-licensed attorney's advice about your facts.

The tax track is a different legal process — the taxing authority must sue and win a court judgment before any sale, and unlike the mortgage track, some eligible former owners have a statutory right to redeem afterward — the deadline and cost depend on the property and the purchaser. If you are 65+ or disabled with a homestead, certain qualifying homeowners may seek a tax-deferral affidavit (Tax Code §33.06) — the tax still accrues with interest, and its effect on a pending case is worth confirming with the tax office or counsel.

HOA foreclosure generally requires a court's approval for residential assessment liens, and many associations must offer a payment-plan option (Prop. Code §209.0062 — exceptions apply by association size and prior defaults). Texas law provides a 180-day redemption period for certain residential association-lien sales (§209.011), measured from the association's required post-sale notice, with strict mail rules. Eligibility and required steps vary — contacting housing counsel or legal aid promptly is one option; the verified numbers are in the calls section of the tool page.
